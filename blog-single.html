1. Introduction & Principles of Asepsis

Surgical site infections (SSIs) remain one of the most preventable causes of post-operative morbidity in small animal practice. Achieving strict surgical asepsis requires more than just wearing clean scrubs; it demands a disciplined chain of aseptic behaviors starting from patient clip preparation to final skin closure.

Veterinary surgeons must distinguish between sanitization, disinfection, and sterilization. In the operating room, any breach in the sterile barrier introduces transient pathogens directly into vascular tissues.

Clinical Insight: The 5-Minute Scrub Rule Initial surgical scrubs of the day must last a full 5 minutes timed by wall clock. Subsequent scrubs between routine cases may be reduced to 3 minutes using alcohol-based chlorhexidine gluconate rubs.

2. Sterile Surgical Scrub Mechanics

The goal of surgical hand scrubbing is to eliminate transient microflora and significantly reduce resident skin bacteria. Two primary scrub techniques are recognized in veterinary medicine:

A. Anatomical Timed Scrub Technique

Dividing each finger, hand, and forearm into four anatomical planes (dorsal, palmar, medial, lateral) ensures zero skin surface is skipped:

  1. Pre-wash: Perform a standard 30-second handwash under warm water to remove macroscopic soil.
  2. Nail cleaning: Use a sterile pick under running water to clean subungual spaces.
  3. Scrub sequence: Scrub fingertips (30 strokes), then each finger surface, palm, back of hand, wrist, and progress up to 2 inches above the elbow.
  4. Rinsing: Keep hands continuously elevated above elbows so water drains from fingertips down toward non-sterile elbows.
Warning: Avoid Over-Scrubbing Abrasions Vigorous scrub brush friction causes epidermal micro-abrasions, releasing deeper resident skin bacteria into the surgical field. Always use soft sponge bristles on skin and firm bristles only for fingernails.

3. Instrument Ergonomics & Mayo Stand Setup

A well-organized Mayo stand accelerates surgical efficiency and reduces open-wound air exposure time. Arrange instruments logically according to procedure phases:

“Organization in the sterile field is not an aesthetic preference—it is a surgical safety protocol that directly impacts operative duration and patient anesthesia risk.”

Mayo Stand Arrangement Checklist

  • Scalpel Handles: No. 3 handle fitted with No. 10 or 15 blades placed on the right border.
  • Tissue Forceps: Adson-Brown or rat-tooth forceps positioned adjacent to incision tools.
  • Hemostatic Forceps: Curved Rochester-Carmalt & Crile forceps grouped by size.
  • Needle Holders: Mayo-Hegar or Olsen-Hegar needle drivers aligned with suture packets.

4. Patient Positioning & Draping Protocols

Proper patient clipping and four-corner field draping establish a clean perimeter around the primary incision site:
